# The Man Who Received a Message From Tomorrow

Genre: Sci-fi, mystery, thriller

At exactly 3:33 AM, Alpha received a message from an unknown number.

“Whatever happens, do not open your front door tomorrow at 9:00 PM.”

There was no name. No phone number. No profile picture.

Just that one sentence.

Alpha smiled and deleted it.

The next morning, he found the same message in his phone.

He deleted it again.

At noon, it returned.

At sunset, it returned once more.

This time, another sentence appeared beneath it.

“You have already died once. You just don't remember.”

Alpha stopped smiling.

He checked his phone for viruses. Nothing. He restarted it. The message remained.

Then he noticed something strange.

The timestamp showed that the message had been sent tomorrow.

October 18, 2026.

But today was October 17.

At 8:59 PM the next evening, Alpha stood inside his apartment, staring at the front door.

He had spent the entire day trying to convince himself that someone was playing a prank.

Then someone knocked.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

His phone vibrated.

“DO NOT OPEN THE DOOR.”

A voice came from the other side.

"Alpha? It's me."

It sounded exactly like him.

Same voice. Same breathing. Same tiny hesitation before speaking.

"Please open the door," the voice said. "We don't have much time."

Alpha stepped backward.

His phone vibrated again.

“If you open the door, the timeline will collapse.”

He typed a question with shaking hands.

Who are you?

The reply appeared instantly.

“I am you, thirty years from now.”

Outside, the knocking stopped.

Then the voice whispered through the door.

"Listen carefully. In thirty seconds, you're going to hear an explosion. When you do, hide beneath the desk. Do not look at the window."

Alpha stared at the screen.

"Why should I believe you?"

The answer came from the other side of the door.

"Because when you were seven years old, you buried a broken watch beneath the mango tree behind your childhood home. You thought your father would be angry if he discovered you had broken it."

Alpha's blood ran cold.

Nobody knew that.

Not even his family.

Then the lights went out.

Ten seconds later, a blinding flash illuminated the window.

An explosion shook the building.

Alpha dropped beneath the desk.

Glass shattered somewhere above him.

He covered his head and waited.

When the silence returned, his phone lit up.

“Good. You survived the first event.”

Alpha typed furiously.

What is happening?

The answer took several seconds.

“Humanity invented a machine capable of predicting the future. Then someone discovered that prediction was not observation. It was creation.”

Another message followed.

“Every future the machine predicted became a possible reality. Eventually, it predicted the end of the world.”

Alpha swallowed.

What does that have to do with me?

The response appeared.

“You built the machine.”

Alpha stared at the words.

Impossible.

He wasn't a scientist. He had never built anything like that.

Then the voice outside spoke again.

"Open the door now."

Alpha froze.

His phone displayed a new warning.

“DO NOT TRUST THE PERSON OUTSIDE.”

But another message appeared beneath it.

“I lied. The explosion wasn't the first event. It was the last.”

Alpha's breathing became shallow.

Two messages.

Two versions of himself.

Both claiming to know the future.

Both telling him what to do.

He looked toward the door.

"Which one of you is real?" he shouted.

For the first time, both voices answered simultaneously.

"I am."

The door handle began to turn.

Alpha grabbed the nearest heavy object and backed into the corner.

The door opened.

Standing in the hallway was a man with Alpha's face, his eyes, and a deep scar running from his forehead to his cheek.

The stranger looked exhausted.

Behind him, the hallway stretched into darkness that seemed to have no end.

"Finally," the stranger whispered.

Alpha raised the object.

"Stay there."

The stranger lifted both hands.

"You have to listen. The machine is already running. In six minutes, this reality will be erased."

"How do I stop it?"

The stranger looked directly into his eyes.

"You don't."

Alpha's stomach dropped.

"Then why did you come?"

The older man smiled sadly.

"Because I need you to do something I couldn't."

He reached into his pocket and produced a small black device.

It looked like an ordinary flash drive.

"Take this to the abandoned laboratory beneath the old railway station. Connect it to the central computer. Then enter a name."

"Whose name?"

The stranger hesitated.

"Mine."

Alpha looked at the device.

Then back at the stranger.

"What is your name?"

The man opened his mouth.

No sound came out.

His face changed.

Fear replaced exhaustion.

"Oh no," he whispered.

The hallway lights began flickering.

His hands started becoming transparent.

"What's happening to you?" Alpha asked.

The stranger grabbed his wrist.

"Listen to me. The machine doesn't predict the future anymore."

His voice cracked.

"It predicts who deserves to exist."

Then he vanished.

The door slammed shut.

Alpha stood alone.

His phone displayed one final message.

“You have six minutes.”

He ran.

Down the stairs. Through the empty street. Past abandoned cars and flickering streetlights.

At the old railway station, he found a staircase leading underground.

At the bottom stood a steel door.

Beyond it was a vast laboratory filled with dead screens, tangled cables, and a single computer still running.

Alpha inserted the device.

A black screen appeared.

IDENTITY REQUIRED.

He typed his own name.

ACCESS DENIED.

He typed the name of the older man.

ACCESS DENIED.

The timer reached thirty seconds.

Then Alpha noticed a small line at the bottom of the screen.

ENTER THE NAME OF THE FIRST PERSON ERASED.

He suddenly remembered the message from the future.

You have already died once. You just don't remember.

His hands trembled as he typed his own name again.

This time, he added one word.

ALPHA — ORIGINAL INSTANCE.

The screen turned white.

A final question appeared.

WHICH VERSION OF REALITY SHOULD SURVIVE?

Two options appeared.

THE WORLD THAT EXISTS.

THE WORLD THAT SHOULD HAVE EXISTED.

Alpha understood.

The machine had created countless versions of reality. Every prediction had become another possible world. Every erased future had once contained real people.

The older man had come from a future where Alpha had sacrificed himself to save everyone.

But something had gone wrong.

The machine had erased that future, too.

Alpha moved the cursor.

He could preserve the world he knew.

Or he could restore every lost possibility.

He thought about the people who had disappeared. The lives that had never been lived. The versions of himself that had died without knowing why.

Then he selected the second option.

THE WORLD THAT SHOULD HAVE EXISTED.

The computer shut down.

Every screen went dark.

And Alpha heard a sound that no human had heard before.

The sound of time reversing.

He opened his eyes.

It was October 17, 2026.

3:33 AM.

He was sitting in his room.

His phone vibrated.

An unknown number.

A new message appeared.

“Whatever happens, do not open your front door tomorrow at 9:00 PM.”

Alpha stared at it.

Then another message arrived.

“This time, remember what you chose.”

He looked out the window.

The stars were different.

And in the reflection on the glass, someone was standing behind him.

Someone with his face.

Someone who smiled and whispered:

"Round two."

THE END.

# The Last Light in the Universe

An original science-fiction mystery story.

At 2:17 AM, the universe went silent.

Not quiet. Silent.

Every star stopped shining. Every planet lost its atmosphere. Every radio signal, every heartbeat, every sound that had ever existed disappeared in a single instant.

Except for one small room on Earth.

Inside that room, a twenty-three-year-old man named Arin sat in front of an old computer, watching a message appear on his screen.

UNKNOWN TRANSMISSION RECEIVED.

Arin had spent years collecting things people had forgotten: old photographs, abandoned websites, forgotten music, broken games, and stories nobody remembered telling. He believed that if something was preserved somewhere, it never truly disappeared.

But the message on his screen was different.

It contained only seven words.

You are the last memory of everything.

Arin stared at the sentence.

Then another message appeared.

Do not look outside.

Naturally, he looked outside.

Beyond his window, the city was frozen beneath a black sky. Buildings stood like lifeless monuments. Cars had stopped in the middle of the roads. Even the moon had vanished.

And where the stars should have been, there was an enormous eye.

It was watching him.

Arin stepped away from the window.

His computer chimed.

We have found you.

"Who are you?" he whispered.

The reply appeared immediately.

We are what remains after the end.

Arin typed with trembling fingers.

"What happened to the universe?"

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the screen filled with thousands of photographs.

Galaxies. Civilizations. Oceans. Forests. Children playing beneath unfamiliar suns. Creatures that had never existed on Earth. Entire worlds that humanity had never discovered.

Every image faded to black.

One by one.

Finally, a sentence appeared.

The universe has reached its final second.

Arin looked at the clock.

2:18 AM.

The second hand was not moving.

He typed, "Why am I still alive?"

The answer was short.

Because you remembered us.

Suddenly, he understood something impossible.

The universe was not a place.

It was a memory.

Every star existed because something had once observed its light. Every civilization existed because something had once known its name. Every life left a tiny mark on reality, and those marks held the universe together.

But time had reached its end.

The memories were disappearing.

And Arin, a man who had spent his life preserving forgotten things, had somehow become the final keeper of existence.

His computer displayed a new option.

RESTORE UNIVERSE?

Below it were two choices.

YES.

NO.

Arin moved the cursor toward YES.

A warning appeared.

Restoration requires one living memory as its foundation.

He stopped.

"What does that mean?"

The reply came slowly.

If you restore everything, you will disappear.

Arin looked around his little room.

His old phone lay beside the keyboard. His photographs were stacked on a shelf. His favorite songs waited inside a folder on his hard drive. Every ordinary thing suddenly felt precious.

He had spent his life afraid of losing things.

Now he was being asked to choose between preserving everything and preserving himself.

He thought about the people he had never met, the children who would never be born, the oceans that would never rise beneath another sunrise.

He thought about all the stories that had not yet been told.

Then he smiled.

"One memory," he said. "That's all you need?"

The computer replied:

One memory that refuses to forget.

Arin clicked YES.

Light erupted from the screen.

For a moment, he saw everything.

The first star igniting in the darkness. The first ocean forming on a newborn planet. The first living creature opening its eyes. The first human looking up at the night sky and wondering whether anyone else existed.

He saw civilizations rise and fall across a billion worlds.

He saw the last star die.

And he saw himself, sitting alone in a dark room, choosing to remember it all.

Then Arin disappeared.

The universe breathed again.

Stars returned to the sky. The moon appeared above the city. Traffic moved. Somewhere, a baby cried. Millions of people woke from strange dreams they would never fully understand.

At 2:19 AM, a man in another country switched on his computer.

A new file had appeared on his desktop.

Its name was THE_LAST_MEMORY.txt.

Inside was a single sentence.

If you can remember something that no longer exists, then perhaps it has never truly died.

The man read it twice.

Then he saved the file.

Far beyond the edges of the observable universe, in a place where time had not yet learned how to begin, a tiny light appeared.

It had no planet.

No star.

No observer.

Yet it shone.

And somewhere inside that light, a voice whispered:

"Let there be something."

The universe began again.

But this time, it remembered that it had once ended.

And it would never be quite the same.

The End.
